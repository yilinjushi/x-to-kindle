The Most Important Formula in AI

Everyone in AI talks about personalization, but almost nobody has figured out how to teach a model your taste without making you hate the process.

Try to train an AI on what good looks like—say, to help you find the next beat in a talk or edit in your voice—and it immediately turns you into a grader. Read these paragraphs. Pick a winner. Label 30 pairs. You close the tab, and the system learns nothing.

The bottleneck used to be intelligence. Now it's human attention: how do you get what's in a person's head into a model when their attention is limited and they hate doing grunt work?

Mike Taylor, our head of evals at Every, and I worked out a formula for what that exchange actually has to optimize. I think it's the most important formula in AI right now—and the formula that, if we get it right, makes a company like Every work.

Here is the whole idea in one sentence: the value of any question an AI asks you equals the odds you'll actually answer it, times how much your answer updates what the model knows about your taste.

Long grading sessions would teach the model a lot, but you close the tab—so the odds you answer drop to zero. Either way, the value is zero.
